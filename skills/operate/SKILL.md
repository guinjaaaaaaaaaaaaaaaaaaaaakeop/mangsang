---
name: operate
description: Use after a slice is built (to relate its concepts, plan sections, code and tests), when a conversation or a person's explanation should become part of the model, when `mangsang impact` reports stale or broken relations, when `check` or `cq` fails, or when a project first adopts mangsang. Declares concepts (the net's nodes), proposes relations with evidence for a human to confirm, and disposes of staleness; never confirms on its own.
---

The engine is `mangsang.py` at this plugin's root: `python3 <plugin root>/mangsang.py <command> --target <project>` (`python` on hosts that have no `python3`).

1. First use — concepts before prose: `register` the plan document, the code modules and the test files, then declare the net's nodes *before* writing plan sections: `concept add NAME --means "one sentence" --by <name>` (or `--delegated`) for each idea the project is about. A plan section is a projection of a concept — write the section after the concept it projects, and relate it (`realizes`). Then declare the net's invariants (they answer to `check`): coverage of plan sections by code and by tests; `resolved`; `projection` — every concept realized in doc, code and test: `{"kind": "projection", "media": {"doc": ["plan/"], "code": ["src/"], "test": ["tests/"]}}`; and the reverse — every plan section belongs to a concept: `{"kind": "coverage", "predicate": "realizes", "anchors": "plan/PLAN.md#Q-*", "as": "src"}` (a section outside every concept is prose leading the net — red until a concept owns it or the person says why not). Then declare the domain questions (they answer to `cq`): what the *product* must answer — `cq add ID --text "the question" --verify '{"kind": "answered-by", "concepts": ["NAME"]}' --by <name>` — the named concept's `means` sentence is the answer; declaring the pairing is a judgment, so it is signed.
2. After a slice: propose relations in a `proposals.json` — `{"relations": [{"src", "predicate", "dst", "evidence"}]}` — the slice's section/symbols/tests each `realizes concept:NAME` (evidence quotes the concept's `means` or the projection's text), plus plan-section↔symbol (`documents`) and test↔symbol (`verifies`) where they say more than the concept does. The engine rejects proposals without evidence, with dead anchors, or outside the vocabulary. Show the list to the person; `confirm proposals.json --by <name>` on their word, or `--delegated "<why>"` (when the orchestrator declared the hand-off once with chongdae's `delegate`, pass that id — `--delegated D-xxxx` — and it is stored as a structured reference instead of a pasted reason). Then `observe --reset`.
3. Before editing a registered file: `lookup <path>` prints the confirmed relations standing on it and marks which ones the edit would make stale. Size the task as the edit *plus* those relation updates — one piece of work, not an edit now and an `impact` surprise later. (Guidance, not a gate: skipping it breaks nothing; `impact` still catches everything after.)
4. `impact` says stale: `impact --show` prints what changed in each — the anchor's text when the relation was confirmed (from git) against now. Read that against the section or test it is related to. If the sentence still holds, `reconfirm <id>… --by|--delegated` — that records what you saw now as `seen`; staleness is judged against `seen`, so `observe --reset` alone clears nothing. If it does not hold, the section or test must change (that is work for a run), then reconfirm under the new text (`--evidence` when the quote moved). Never reconfirm without reading.
5. `impact` says broken: the anchor was renamed or removed. If the *concept* is what moved (a section retitle, a symbol rename), the concept survives — `retire <id> --why` the broken projection and propose it again under the new anchor; the net's node needs no change. `concept rename` when the *name* itself changes; `concept revise --means` when the *meaning* changes (every projection goes stale — that is the net leading the documents, not following them).
6. `check` red is the net's health (a lint, not a question): FAILED (a declared invariant does not hold — propose the missing relations, step 2), UNASKABLE (the net moved out from under the invariant — its predicate or anchors are gone; `cq revise` it, with the person's word), UNWATCHED (the net holds something no invariant watches — declare one or tell the person why not).
7. `cq` red is the domain layer: FAILED (the answering concept is unrealized, or its projections moved since confirmation — re-read the concept against the code before trusting the sentence; then reconfirm or revise), UNANSWERABLE (the concept a question names is gone — the model cannot answer this of the domain; declare the concept or retire the question, with the person's word), UNQUESTIONED (a concept no question names — the model holds a meaning nobody asks for; write the question or say why not). Questions and invariants are declarations alike: `cq add/revise/retire` records who asked, refuses one the current model cannot support, and keeps retired ones in the record. Hand-editing cq.json records nothing — prefer the commands.

mangsang is optional for a project; when it is absent, whatever it would have watched is a non-claim, not a substitute.
6. From a conversation (a planning talk, an interview, someone explaining their domain) — the model is built from what was said, so the record must show who said what and what was approved on seeing what:
   - **Before the first source**, ask two things: the name the person signs and speaks under (never derive one from an email, an account or session metadata), and whether their words may be committed to the repository — a source is a committed record. Write the name into `mangsang/people.json` (`{"people": ["<name>"], "agents": ["Claude ("]}`): from then on the engine refuses any other signature.
   - **Keep both sides.** Every turn that matters is a source, whoever said it: the person's explanation, and your own summary, reading or clarifying question when it shapes the model — speaker `Claude (<model>)`, the host's model name. A person's short answer ("yes, both", "go with that") is kept with `--replies-to <the turn it answers>`; without it the answer means nothing to a later reader. Take the words from the host's own session record, not from memory: `source add ID --from-transcript <session file> --match "a phrase only that turn contains" [--kind person|agent|question|answer]` (on Claude Code the session file is `~/.claude/projects/<the project path, / as ->/<CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID>.jsonl`); it keeps the turn verbatim, names your turn by your model, and fills the locator. Only when there is no transcript (a meeting, a pasted text) pass the words on stdin (`--file -`) with `--locator` saying where they were said.
   - **Verbatim only.** A transcript you were given, never a summary in its place, never text you filled in. A correction is a new source; the old one stays.
   - **Propose, then declare.** Show the concept names with their `means` sentences and the question list first; declare after the person approves, `--by <their name> --approved-in source:<the turn where they said yes>` (kept as a source first); without such a turn, sign `--delegated`. A broad request ("make a model of this") is not a delegation of each meaning. A `means` sentence you wrote is grounded in the source where you wrote it and in the person's reply approving it — two relations, both quoting — so the record shows the words came from you and the approval from them.
   - **Unanswered is a question**: `cq add ID --text "..." --verify '{"kind": "open"}'` — OPEN until a concept carries the answer, then `cq revise ID --verify '{"kind": "answered-by", ...}'`. Two sources that disagree are not yours to settle: show both quotes and ask.
   - **A model made only of conversation** has no doc/code/test media. Declare the invariant that fits it — `cq add grounded --text "is every concept grounded in what was said?" --verify '{"kind": "projection", "media": {"said": ["source:"]}}'` — which also watches `realizes`; do not leave `check` reporting UNWATCHED with no reason recorded.
   - **Showing the model** is a local file: `report --html NET.html` — one self-contained page whose graph (concepts, projections, questions) draws in any browser, offline, nothing installed — and give the path (on macOS, `open NET.html` shows it). `report --out NET.md` is the same page as Markdown. Do not publish it anywhere — a hosted page, a share link — unless the person asks for that. The page is derived: regenerate it, do not commit it.
